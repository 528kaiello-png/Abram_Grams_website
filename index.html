Abram Games

Born: July 29, 1914

Died: August 27, 1996

So what is Abram Games known for?

Abram Games was an influential British graphic designer and artist renowned for his landmark wartime and post-war poster designs. He pioneered a striking modernist aesthetic guided by his famous design philosophy: "Maximum meaning, minimum means."

Your Britain: Fight For It Now ▼

Year: 1942

Client: Army Bureau of Current Affairs (ABCA)

Medium: Lithograph Poster

About: Created during WWII to inspire soldiers by contrasting modern British social housing developments against derelict slums. It exemplifies Games' philosophy of combining symbolic imagery into a single stark message.

Join the ATS ("Blonde Bombshell") ▼

Year: 1941

Client: Auxiliary Territorial Service (ATS)

Medium: Recruitment Poster

About: One of Games' most controversial posters. Dubbed the "Blonde Bombshell", it was withdrawn after Parliamentary debate for being "too glamorous" for military recruitment, though it remains a landmark of 20th-century graphic design.

Festival of Britain Emblem ▼

Year: 1951

Client: Festival of Britain Committee

Medium: National Brand Identity / Graphic Symbol

About: Known as the "Festival Star", this emblem featured Britannia's head atop a compass star with bunting flags. It was featured on millions of souvenirs, posters, coins, and banners across post-war Britain.

BBC Television Symbol ▼

Year: 1953

Client: BBC (British Broadcasting Corporation)

Medium: Animated On-Air Identity

About: Affectionately nicknamed "The Bat's Wings", this was the BBC's first animated television symbol. It combined a globe, flashing eye, and revolving wings symbolizing international broadcasting power.

Guinness Advertisements ▼

Year: 1956

Client: Guinness Brewery

Medium: Commercial Advertising

About: Games created striking minimalist advertisements for Guinness, combining the iconic dark pint silhouette with bold geometric shapes to convey energy and strength without unnecessary visual clutter.

Use Arms Efficiently ▼

Year: 1940

Client: War Office

Medium: Military Safety Poster

About: Designed during Games' tenure as Official War Poster Designer. The poster visually merges soldier weaponry with mechanical precision, embodying his famous motto: "Maximum Meaning, Minimum Means".

Jersey Umbrella Poster ▼

Year: 1951

Client: Jersey Tourism Office

Medium: Tourism Advertisement

About: Features a multi-colored beach umbrella whose handle curves seamlessly to form the letter "J", while the remaining letters "ersey" cast inside the shadow. Uses flat colors and graphic wit to evoke a peaceful summer seaside escape.

Freedom From Hunger ▼

Year: 1960

Client: United Nations (FAO)

Medium: Humanitarian Campaign Poster

About: A famous international campaign poster designed for the UN's Food and Agriculture Organization. An empty head of wheat doubles as a starving human profile, demonstrating Games' power to blend dual symbols into a single striking image.

Use Spades Not Ships ▼

Year: 1942

Client: Ministry of Agriculture / War Office

Medium: WWII Home Front Poster

About: Encouraged home gardening to reduce reliance on food imports during World War II. The clever graphic turns a garden spade cutting into the soil into the bow of a merchant supply vessel.

See Britain by Train ▼

Year: 1951

Client: British Railways

Medium: Travel & Transport Poster

About: Commissioned for British Railways to encourage post-war domestic travel. Games fused railway track lines seamlessly with rolling green countryside shapes to invite citizens to explore Britain by rail.
